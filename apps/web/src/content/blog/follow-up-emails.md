People miss emails. They read them on a phone between meetings, mean to answer later and never do. That's why a lot of replies come from follow-ups, not from the first email.

But "just bumping this up" four times isn't a follow-up strategy. Here's one that respects the person on the other end.

## A simple cadence

Four touches over about two weeks is plenty for most offers.

| Touch | When | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First email | Day 1 | The specific reason you're writing, and one clear ask |
| Follow-up 1 | Day 3 | A short nudge that adds one new thing |
| Follow-up 2 | Day 7 | A different angle: a result, a resource, a question |
| The last one | Day 14 | A polite close that makes it easy to say no |

Send them in the same thread, during business hours in the prospect's time zone, and space them out so they don't land like an alarm.

## Every follow-up adds something new

A follow-up that only repeats the first email is a reminder that you were ignored. Give them a new reason to answer instead.

> **Follow-up 1:** One more thought: most teams we talk to after opening a new market say the slow part is finding the right partners, not closing them. Is that true for you in Lisbon?

> **Follow-up 2:** Put together a short list of the partner types that worked for teams entering Portugal. Want me to send it over?

## Make the last email easy to answer

The last touch is often the one that gets a reply, because it takes the pressure off.

> **The last one:** I haven't heard back, so I'll assume the timing's off and stop here. If partners in Lisbon become a priority, just reply to this email and I'll pick it up.

Then actually stop.

## Know when to quit

Most of what makes follow-ups annoying is sending them after you should have stopped:

- **Stop the moment they reply.** Even "not now" is an answer, and the sequence shouldn't keep going after it.
- **Stop for their coworkers too.** If someone at the company answers, you probably don't want the rest of their team getting your follow-up an hour later.
- **Pause on out-of-office.** An auto-reply isn't a reply. Wait until they're back.
- **Respect unsubscribes everywhere**, not just in the campaign where they clicked.
